I learned Arabic calligraphy the slow way: hundreds of repetitions of a single letter before you're allowed to join two. It turned out to be the best interface-design training I've had.

## Rhythm before ornament

A line of good calligraphy has a pulse — thick to thin, pressure to release. Interfaces have the same pulse: dense where it matters, open where it can breathe. When a screen feels off, it's usually arrhythmic before it's ugly.

## Negative space is the composition

In calligraphy, the white *between* the strokes is not leftover — it's drawn as deliberately as the ink. The same discipline catches most layout problems: stop adding marks, and start shaping the space around them.

## One confident stroke

The reed pen punishes hesitation; a wavering line shows every doubt. Interfaces reward the same conviction — one clear decision, committed to fully, beats five tentative ones hedged against each other.

None of this is metaphor for its own sake. It's the same skill — composition under constraint — applied to a different surface.
